        How it works

        Left alone

        The agent gets a body on a strip of 24 columns, a few cubes, a rock and a ring, and the line "There is nothing you have to do." It plays three sessions of ten turns, up to five actions a turn. Between sessions the island resets and its context is wiped. Only its NOTES and LOG survive.

        Checked by a real engine

        Every action runs in a small deterministic physics engine in this page. The island hides four things its manual never mentions: a hard rock impact makes new cubes, a throw made mid-jump goes higher, a jump climbs higher than a step, and cubes make stairs. The engine notices when the agent does one, whatever the agent says.

        The test

        Then the same model gets two goals: build towers (six cubes only make two) and land a cube on the Spire without climbing. It plays them with empty notes, with its own notes, and with one technique erased. A miniature of Is this machine playing? (Cloos et al., 2026). Two runs per arm is an anecdote, not a statistic.
